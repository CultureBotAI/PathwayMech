methylglyoxal degradation I

PathwayMech / Browse pathways / MetaCyc:PWY-5386

MetaCyc:PWY-5386

The complete three-reaction glutathione-dependent route from methylglyoxal to pyruvate represented by MetaCyc PWY-5386 and PathBank SMP0002125, curated for the E. coli K-12 protein core. GloA acts as a homodimer on the spontaneously formed methylglyoxal-glutathione hemithioacetal; the source net reaction includes that nonenzymatic condensation. Monomeric GloB hydrolyzes (R)-S-lactoylglutathione, regenerating glutathione and releasing D-(R)-lactate. FAD-dependent membrane-associated Dld oxidizes D-lactate to pyruvate while reducing a quinone. Exact Rhea quinone/quinol classes are retained without asserting ubiquinone-6 specificity; ubiquinone-8 supports reconstituted E. coli respiration. The native PathBank L-lactate identity and oxidized-glutathione cross-reference are rejected. Pathway-level flux evidence comes from MG1655-derived SIJ488 strains with engineered glycolytic deletions grown on glycerol, not unrestricted wild-type flux. The older MetaCyc description calls the D-lactate fate likely; the 2022 deletion study supports this branch in its tested context. The GloB branch does not exclude accessory GloC activity. Ni activates GloA whereas Zn activates GloB; published GloA metal occupancies differ and are retained in the cofactor evidence. Upstream methylglyoxal synthesis, alternative glutathione-independent routes, Kef regulation and downstream respiratory-chain reactions are outside this pathway.
