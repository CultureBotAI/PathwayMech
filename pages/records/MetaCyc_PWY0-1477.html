ethanolamine utilization

MetaCyc:PWY0-1477

Ethanolamine utilization as defined by MetaCyc:PWY0-1477 contains five reactions and two acetaldehyde branches. Adenosylcobalamin-dependent ethanolamine ammonia-lyase produces acetaldehyde and ammonium. The oxidative branch passes through acetyl-CoA and acetyl phosphate to acetate with ATP production; the reductive branch produces ethanol. The native pathway selects left-to-right traversal of ETHAMLY-RXN, ACETALD-DEHYDROG-RXN and PHOSACETYLTRANS-RXN, and right-to-left traversal of ACETATEKIN-RXN and ALCOHOL-DEHYDROG-RXN. The latter four reactions have reversible own-direction annotations. All 22 core material occurrences and four ordering relations are retained. These directions and orderings do not establish a branch ratio, molecular channeling, a whole-route net equation or physiological flux. RXN-19336 is ancillary EutT cofactor chemistry, outside the five-member pathway boundary.

The MetaCyc pathway narrative describes ethanolamine as a carbon, nitrogen and energy source in enteric bacteria and intestinal phosphatidylethanolamine as a substrate source. It compares the protein-shell eut metabolosome with carboxysomes and propanediol microcompartments, and attributes aldehyde-toxicity protection, reduced acetaldehyde volatilization and cofactor recycling to compartmentalization. It reports full eut operons in Enterobacteriaceae, Firmicutes, Nocardioides and Fusobacterium nucleatum, and shorter operons in other organisms that may encode only ethanolamine ammonia-lyase. These are source-attributed ecological and comparative statements, not additional directly demonstrated taxa for this record. The studied Salmonella operon contains 17 genes, including five shell-protein homologues (PMID:10464203). Similarity to another compartment or possession of a short operon does not establish the complete route.

The two declared strains are the native pathway's direct associations: Escherichia coli K-12 MG1655 and Salmonella enterica serovar Typhimurium LT2. They are not a claim that every experiment measured intact-route flux in those strains. The 17 E. coli UniProt proteins have broader K-12 taxon 83333 scope; their curator caution about CPZ-55 and reduced eut expression is retained as an inferred expression limitation, not proof of pathway absence. Native META and ECOLI objects and individual UniProt proteins remain separate issuing identities. Non-K12 isolates, JM109, BL21 expression hosts, yeast and engineered BW25113-derived strains are identified with their experiments. Contextual expression patterns in iModulonDB support coordinated expression only, not catalysis, transport, binding or native pathway flux.

Adenosylcobalamin is the EutBC cofactor and, with ethanolamine, a signal for eut-operon induction. MetaCyc describes E. coli as requiring an external corrinoid source and Salmonella as capable of de novo synthesis under anaerobic conditions; those source statements are not universal growth assertions for every strain or medium. EutR genetic reporter and induction experiments support control of operon transcription (DOI:10.1128/JB.174.20.6634-6643.1992). Autoinduction and competition between EutR and EutBC for adenosylcobalamin remain a regulatory model; the 1994 account was assessed at abstract and introductory scope (DOI:10.1128/JB.176.5.1287-1296.1994). Induction does not directly demonstrate physical ligand binding. K-12 EutR assignments largely transfer by similarity. An exact issued operon or transcription-process target is not bound here, so transcriptional regulation is retained in narrative rather than substituted with activation of a metabolic reaction. The 2004 EutT study found elevated adenosyltransferase activity after LT2 eutT overexpression in E. coli extracts, with cobA/promoter and cyanocobalamin conditions relevant to interpretation. It distinguishes adenosylation from EutA protection/reactivation; it is not homogeneous-protein or direct EutT-to-EAL delivery evidence (PMID:15516577).

EutBC whole-reaction catalysis is assigned to the native heteromeric complex. Both the separately issued META and ECOLI complexes specify six EutB and six EutC chains in their component slots. Their prose description of a hexamer of (alpha beta)2 dimers is ambiguous relative to those coefficients and is not silently repaired. The alpha6/beta6 structural account and activity of an N-terminally truncated EutC preparation were assessed at abstract scope (PMID:19762342). Native evidence also includes E. coli NCIB 8114 and JM109 preparations, which are not MG1655 experiments. The four UniProt EutB/C activity annotations are retained only as subunit contributions in the heteromer, with their direct, probable or HAMAP evidence tiers; neither subunit independently catalyzes the complete cleavage reaction. EutC truncation and peptide-targeting constructs are separate experimental states.

Native activity-level inhibition and activation statements retain their exact molecular-activity targets and assay scope. EutBC inhibitors and EutD pyruvate activation are distinct from EutR transcriptional control and EutA reactivation. The ethanolaminium source also contains three off-route regulation backlinks involving serine deaminase, beta-galactosidase and a methyltransferase; these are context, not eut-operon regulation or additional pathway members. Shared reaction backlinks likewise do not make all linked enzymes ethanolamine catalysts: seven activity assignments are selected, eight are adjacent context and 56 are unrelated to this pathway selection.

EutD phosphate acetyltransferase is distinct from general Pta. The native E. coli complex has two chains. Recombinant LT2 H6-EutD expressed in BL21(DE3) was assayed in 2004 in the acetyl-phosphate plus CoA to acetyl-CoA direction; isolated plasmid EutD also rescued a pta acs acetate-growth defect in JE7245 with 50 mM acetate and 250 micromolar arabinose (PMID:14996820). That is not direct forward-direction kinetics or proof of native shell permeability. The 2016 P/Q study separately used EutD to generate acetyl phosphate from acetyl-CoA and orthophosphate in coupled assays (PMID:26448059). E. coli EutD activity in both directions has additional abstract-level support (PMID:21046341). LT2 eutD deletion phenotypes depend on inoculum washing and medium carryover; intact-shell acetate tests differ from isolated-EutD complementation. EutD is neither universally essential nor universally dispensable on the basis of these experiments (PMID:23585538). UniProt explicitly leaves cytoplasmic versus microcompartment placement uncertain for E. coli EutD; the LT2 homodimer annotation is a similarity assignment.

The older pathway narrative calls the EutE acetylating acetaldehyde dehydrogenase function assumed. Individual evidence is more specific: the native E. coli activity record concerns unpurified heterologous yeast activity, whereas LT2 N-terminal His6-EutE expressed in BL21(DE3) RIL ZH103 was approximately 90% pure and showed acetylating aldehyde-dehydrogenase and acetyl-CoA-reductase activities with NAD(H) preference. Alcohol-dehydrogenase activity was not detected in the tested assays (PMID:21803884). Engineered MG1655 glucose fermentation does not demonstrate native ethanolamine flux. Separate abstract-level studies concern EutE replacing acetyl-CoA synthetase in engineered yeast and EutE/MhpF-assisted yeast acetyl-CoA production (PMID:24269999; PMID:26384570). Those engineering contexts are not additional native-route or localization experiments. NAD is a reaction cosubstrate, not automatically an EutE-bound prosthetic group.

The ethanol branch remains part of the native route. Its LT2 EutG activity record uses conditional mutant-block evidence, while the LT2 UniProt assignment and iron association are inferred. The K-12-derived eutG allele from JCL16/BW25113 was overexpressed with pAL158 in AL626 and tested in crude cell extracts, with nonzero controls (PMID:22731523). At 25 mM aldehyde, 0.2 mM NAD(P)H and 37 degrees C, acetaldehyde activities were 0.56 ± 0.12 with NADH and 0.46 ± 0.08 with NADPH; isobutyraldehyde activities were 0.44 ± 0.09 and 0.45 ± 0.05 micromol/min/mg total protein, respectively. The error estimates are standard deviations from triplicates. These similar measured activities do not establish equal purified-enzyme cofactor preference, native LT2 activity or a physiological branch ratio. Some EutG shell-suppression observations in the 2013 cofactor model were unpublished; the NMR products do not establish a universal route ratio (PMID:23585538). Exact alternative aldehyde/NADPH activity microstates were not bound to an additional reaction here, so those measured substrate facts remain narrative rather than an invented equation.

EutP and EutQ have recombinant LT2 acetate-kinase activity in both assayed directions, with EutD coupling providing separate support for acetyl-phosphate generation (PMID:26448059). The proteins were expressed in BL21(lambdaDE3) and assayed after affinity-tag cleavage; a residual Gly-Thr sequence before the initiating methionine is explicitly documented for EutQ and is not assigned to EutP. Both accepted GTP alternative-input assertions preserve those preparation conditions. They do not alter the native ATP/ADP equation or assert an unmeasured GDP output. The native ACETATEKIN-RXN EC 2.7.2.15 conflicts with its acetate chemistry and independently assessed acetate kinase EC 2.7.2.1; the raw EC annotation remains attributable source text. K-12 EutP/Q functions transfer by similarity, not by direct transfer of the LT2 experiment to MG1655.

EutP/Q activity was reported unchanged after EDTA dialysis, with the result described as data not shown. The documented kinetic assays nevertheless contained 6.6 mM MgCl2, and HPLC assays contained 1 mM MgCl2 (PMID:26448059, Results P13-P14 and Methods P39-P40). Thus EDTA-treatment resistance under magnesium-containing assay conditions is supported; magnesium-independent catalysis is not established. The stronger UniProt statement that divalent cations are unnecessary is retained as a database assertion, not adopted as the mechanistic conclusion. Added assay magnesium also does not by itself establish a bound-magnesium cofactor requirement.

The EutQ deletion growth defect was conditional on anoxic 30 mM ethanolamine plus 40 mM tetrathionate after a 0.5 mM glycerol starter; 2.5 mM oxidized glutathione rescue and the absence of the EutP phenotype were condition-specific (PMID:26448059). ATP recycling to EutA/EutT and the location of P/Q remain proposed or unresolved. Internal differences in the paper's eutQ deletion allele number (1182 versus 1183) and reported Q Km (0.70 versus 0.5 mM) are retained as source discrepancies. Held, Kolb, Perdue and colleagues (2016; PMID:27063436; DOI:10.1038/srep24359) reported tagged EutQ/EutM co-elution and assembly phenotypes. C-terminal His6 EutQ behavior differs from N-terminal tagging and truncation. A cytosol-facing EutM interaction is a model; attempted in-vivo mutant validation was limited by instability and stress. These findings do not establish fixed native EutQ position, direct cargo transfer or a physical-interaction edge using an unrelated predicate.

EutA reactivates tested inactive ethanolamine ammonia-lyase in vitro (PMID:15466038). The exact EcoCyc EutA record links EcoCyc:ETHAMLY-CPLX, whose own comment reciprocally describes reactivation. This target remains separate from MetaCyc:ETHAMLY-CPLX. The selected assertion is limited to purified recombinant EutA from JM109 and ethanolamine-inactivated recombinant holoEAL prepared from JM109/pUSI2ENd, with 20 mM ATP, 20 mM MgCl2, 10 micromolar free adenosylcobalamin, pH 8 and 37 degrees C. The apoEAL preparation was attributed to unpublished work; exact identity between every assay sequence and a native database sequence is not claimed. Activation of a cyanocobalamin-bound model complex is a separate reported experiment, not the same inactive holoEAL state. Free ATP, magnesium and adenosylcobalamin required in the assay are not automatically EutA-bound cofactors. ATP-dependent cofactor exchange remains mechanistic interpretation, distinct from EutT adenosylation. LT2 EutA reactivation annotations partly transfer by similarity.

EutH supports membrane entry under conditions of low calculated uncharged ethanolamine below approximately 25 micromolar, including the pH-dependent phenotype and a functional peripheral EutH-Yfp reporter in the LT2-derived experimental system (PMID:15466042). The primary interpretation concerns neutral ethanolamine, not proven transport of its protonated form, and is not purified transport kinetics. Neutral CHEBI:16000 is distinct from the native +1 ETHANOL-AMINE/CHEBI:57603 ethanolaminium reaction participant. Database protonated-substrate wording, including historical iModulonDB context, is conflicting annotation. The selected EcoCyc inner-membrane location is retained without inventing a neutral-substrate transport reaction, proton exchange or direct delivery into the metabolosome. The K-12 transport assignment is a similarity inference and does not turn the Salmonella EutH-Yfp experiment into a K-12 experiment.

EutK, EutL, EutM, EutN and EutS are the supported shell-protein identities. Native component slots and scoped LT2 shell assignments are retained separately from whole-shell stoichiometry. EutL is a three-chain trimer with pseudohexamer geometry, not a six-chain complex. EutM and EutS six-chain source assemblies do not set their copy numbers in a whole shell. EutK is monomeric in the reported solution preparation; its helix-turn-helix shape does not prove nucleic-acid binding. EutN's native/crystal six-chain form and solution pentamer remain different, conflicting preparation scopes; a definitive physiological vertex stoichiometry is not established. Crystal pore geometry and zinc-soak opening do not identify physiological transported solutes or prove zinc-dependent gating, ATP or cobalamin passage. Several LT2 architecture statements transfer from K-12 or other orthologs rather than direct LT2 structural experiments.

Coexpression of LT2 eutSMNLK in engineered E. coli produces artificial shells; EutS-only shells and EutJ-plus-EutS overexpression phenotypes are construct-dependent assembly observations (PMID:22428024; PMID:27063436). They do not establish universal native shell requirements. The approximately 40-degree bent EutS architecture, its G39V altered geometry and terminal-tag effects are distinguished; a flat mutant is not the wild-type assembly. Native full-length cargo localization or transport specificity does not follow from engineered shell formation. EutJ has proposed chaperone, protection or refolding roles and an assembly phenotype, but no supported catalytic reaction or direct EutBC activation is assigned; K-12 functional statements are similarity transfers.

The selected localization experiment concerns the first 19 residues of LT2 EutC fused to EGFP, not full-length EutC. The primary Methods motif MDQKQIEEIVRSVMASMGQ matches UniProt P19265 residues 1-19 and taxon 99287. Antibody access after breaking native Salmonella microcompartment shells supports internal encapsulation of that reporter; the engineered E. coli shell arm is kept separate (PMID:22428024). A K-12 EutG(1-19) reporter failed under its tested construct conditions, which does not exclude native EutG from the compartment. Separate EutC(1-19) and EutE(1-21) targeting claims have abstract-level support (PMID:27450681); they do not demonstrate the position or direct binding partners of every native full-length protein.

The 2013 external/surface EutBC placement was explicitly untested (PMID:23585538). The 2022 immunogold and association experiments with induced LT2-derived JE6583 and deletion controls support EAL/EutA/EutT association with metabolosomes; EAL/EutA lumen placement is an interpretation of those observations, while the EutT side remains unclear (PMID:35785499). The association assays used 20 mM ribose, 30 mM ethanolamine and 200 nM cyanocobalamin. In the 90 mM ethanolamine growth experiments, EutT requirement differed with 10 versus 200 nM cyanocobalamin. EutA-EAL association also had in-vitro biochemical support; EutT partners were not detected under the tested conditions, which is not proof of absence. No fixed EutA:EAL stoichiometry, delivery interaction or universal growth rule follows. Native EcoCyc EutA membrane and EutG membrane/cytosol annotations coexist with UniProt microcompartment inferences. These anatomy statements do not describe a phospholipid membrane around the protein-shell metabolosome. No exclusive universal lumen or exterior location is inferred from the root narrative, a GO component name or reporter targeting alone.

Altered expression and culture conditions allowed growth without an intact metabolosome and conditional EutA/EutE bypass in the 2005 LT2 experiments (30 mM ethanolamine, 150 nM cyanocobalamin, 2.5 mM GSSG; microplate tests included 0.5 mM glycerol). Shell and accessory-gene phenotypes depend on medium, inoculum carryover, pH, gas exchange and aldehyde loss. The 2005 modified conditions use oxidized glutathione (GSSG) in Methods while Discussion uses GSH terminology; these are not silently made chemically identical (PMID:16291677). The 2006 work supports context-dependent acetaldehyde conservation and shell phenotypes (PMID:16585748). Its EutD phenotype conflicts with the cited Starai 2005 account; a background explanation is tentative, that cited main was not read here, and later washing experiments do not prove a universal reconciliation. The 2013 private-cofactor model combines experiments with hypotheses and unpublished observations, and its Table 7 symbol legend conflicts with Table 5 and surrounding prose; underlying assays, not repaired symbols, control the interpretation (PMID:23585538). Its ethanol-utilization tests implicate AdhP rather than EutG, without making AdhP a newly selected ethanolamine catalyst. Cofactor recycling, aldehyde protection and ATP recycling are therefore retained with condition and model limits rather than imposed on every organism or shell state.

EutT corrinoid adenosyltransferase chemistry is ancillary. The root's older EC 2.5.1.17 attribution differs from dedicated native EutT EC 2.5.1.154. RXN-19336 has nine distinct material-occurrence slots. It assigns native coefficients of two to ATP, cob(II)alamin and water on the input side, and to adenosylcobalamin, diphosphate, orthophosphate and protons on the product side. Reduced and oxidized flavoproteins remain generic source classes with omitted native coefficients. UniProt/Rhea's three-product-proton equation conflicts with the native two-proton annotation. No balanced replacement or exact physiological donor is inferred. Native CPLX-9275 has an omitted component coefficient; separate UniProt dimer support cannot fill it silently. The 2006 study's abstract reports approximately 70%-enriched EutT, diphosphate plus phosphate products by 31P-NMR and extract/FMN/NADH regeneration, but its full specificity experiments were not accessible (PMID:16636051).

EutT metal evidence distinguishes zinc copurification, activity after reconstitution and a proposed physiological ferroprotein (PMID:24336938). The selected FeII assertion is restricted to the tested tag-cleaved LT2 JE6583 EutT expressed in BL21 and reconstituted in vitro. Maximal FeII-reconstituted activity does not establish exclusive physiological iron occupancy or rule out other metal-supported states. Free dihydroflavin/Fre/NADH assay chemistry does not identify a native bound flavoprotein electron donor. K-12 EutT cofactor, catalytic and dimer annotations transfer by similarity. Neither metal stimulation nor a generic reduced-flavoprotein class proves direct EutT-to-EAL delivery.

EutT cobinamide specificity remains oxidation-state dependent and partly conflicting. The 2015 main reports adenosylation of pre-reduced Co(I) cobinamide in bioassays, whose supporting information was unavailable (PMID:25914129, P9). Its Figure 4 competition experiment measures adenosylcobalamin formation and alone does not identify adenosylcobinamide product. Co(II) cobinamide binds the EutT/MgATP system while retaining its axial solvent ligand, unlike the interpreted four-coordinate Co(II) cobalamin state; this is not evidence of no cobinamide binding. The native explicit Co(I)-cobinamide inactivity assertion remains in conflict. Positive pre-reduced Co(I) activity does not establish the UniProt Co(II)-starting equation or a K-12 experiment. The 2006 main and 2015 supporting experiments were not inspected. No new cobinamide reaction is invented, and direct EutT-to-EAL cofactor handoff remains the 2015 proposed model, not an established transfer edge.

Source names, organism ownership, class membership, reaction crosslinks and experimental evidence have different meanings. The native root and five reaction originals define the route; Rhea chemistry correspondence does not make separately issued enzymes or reactions aliases. The 14 core native material identities were independently compared with ChEBI, but generic placeholders in five native chemical-diagram SMILES are not exact structural authority. Narrative inferences, database similarity assertions, abstract-only evidence, retained publisher text and fully accessible primary originals remain distinct evidence tiers. Conditional experimental findings are not whole-route claims, and the inspected literature is not claimed to exhaust the biological evidence.
