# Curcumin degradation: complete two-step CurA route

The new record is `data/pathways/curcumin-degradation.yaml`, grounded in **GO:0036040 (curcumin catabolic process)**. GO's exact synonym “curcumin degradation” and its definition reference PMID:21467222 match the experimentally defined route. MetaCyc **PWY0-1527** and PathBank **SMP0001910 / PW001896.owl** independently delimit the same two ordered reactions. This is the complete reductive route from curcumin through dihydrocurcumin (DHC) to tetrahydrocurcumin (THC), not an assertion that every possible curcumin utilization pathway is represented.

## Source and experimental scope

The full [2011 primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3080977/?report=xml), including its Results and Discussion, was retrieved and read. Figure 4 was separately downloaded and visually inspected; it shows both reductions by CurA, NADPH consumption in each, and the intervening DHC structure. The paper initially screened fecal microorganisms, then explicitly switched to **E. coli K-12 DH10B** for enzyme purification and gene identification. Detailed enzyme characterization used cloned CurA expressed in a BL21 host. This resolves the prior abstract-only uncertainty; the fecal isolate is not mislabeled as K-12.

The record uses **NCBITaxon:83333, E. coli K-12**, matching reviewed UniProt **P76113** (release 2026_03; curA/yncB, b1449/JW5907). Native MetaCyc annotates MG1655. These source scopes are kept distinct: neither a direct MG1655 flux measurement nor activity in every E. coli isolate is asserted. The protein crosswalk is the explicit native **G6760-MONOMER → P76113** unification, reinforced by current UniProt; an inferred family match is not used as the identity bridge.

The public MetaCyc XML explicitly reports version **22.5**; it is not presented as the newest licensed release. Exact response bodies and acquisition times are hashed in `curcumin-source-manifest.json`. Europe PMC full-text XML failed, BioC returned an HTML error, and supplementary PDF endpoints returned a challenge/403. These are retained as access failures, not mistaken for inspected source content. The main article and figure were successfully available. Supplement-specific numerical results are not claimed independently inspected.

## Chemistry, assembly and edge decisions

The selected directed reactions are **RHEA:34821** and **RHEA:34813**, each in the **right-to-left orientation of its Rhea master** (34819 and 34811). Native MetaCyc reactions RXN0-6676 and RXN0-6677 encode the same physiological reductions left to right. Direction is therefore interpreted relative to each source's equation, not transferred mechanically between databases. Rhea explicitly links each selected reaction to the corresponding native reaction.

Both steps retain all five chemical-side entries: substrate, NADPH(4-) and hydron consumed; reduced product and NADP(3-) produced. All coefficients are one. The exact Rhea forms balance both elements and charge. PathBank's NADPH/NADP acid accessions remain in the complete original projection, while the graph uses Rhea's explicit anions; those are formal reaction species, not an experimental measurement of protonation under the paper's assay conditions. No acid-to-anion exact identity is claimed. ChEBI supplies the E,E curcumin and E-DHC structures; the inspected mass/UV product identification is not promoted to a separate full stereochemical determination. The displayed primary pathway structure agrees with this chemical sequence.

The sum equals **RHEA:34817**, whose master 34815 explicitly denotes a multistep reaction. That redundant net reaction is excluded; both components and the intermediate are retained. The full native pathway boundary is therefore covered without double counting. Further CurA reduction to hexahydro- or octahydrocurcumin was not observed in the reported experiments. Unknown uptake and downstream THC fate do not justify invented transporter, mineralization or onward edges.

Physical catalyst **MetaCyc:CPLX0-7927** contains two G6760-MONOMER subunits. The native coefficient, reviewed UniProt homodimer annotation and primary gel-filtration/SDS-PAGE assignment agree. PathBank independently encodes a two-subunit ProteinComplex/3920 with P76113 and controls both reactions. The graph uses one independently identified native complex for both steps; it asserts neither monomer sufficiency nor identity between arbitrary source graph objects.

NADPH's cofactor role is directly supported by the dialysis/addition experiment and is also represented as a consumed redox cosubstrate. It is not called a permanently bound prosthetic group. Main-text metal analysis and inhibition/activation discussion, together with reviewed UniProt, oppose the historical “predicted Zn-dependent” name; no metal cofactor edge is added. Cytosol localization is retained only on the protein, through the native explicit GO:0005829 annotation, with its **EV-COMP-AINF** grade visible. It is not relabeled as direct experimental localization.

Other assay substrates, thiol probes, proposed binding residues, C247A, pH/temperature optima and broad family assignments are source context, not additional pathway branches or regulation. The provisional EC 1.3.1.n3 is not made a graph node. Every such exclusion is accounted for in `curcumin-source-projection.json`; the complete native BioPAX object projection remains separately available.
