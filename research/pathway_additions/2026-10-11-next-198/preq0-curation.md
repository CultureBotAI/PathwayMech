# Complete preQ0 biosynthesis

The accepted record is **GO:0097288, 7-cyano-7-deazaguanine biosynthetic process**, in E. coli K-12. Its complete four-transformation boundary is independently defined by original MetaCyc 22.5 **PWY-6703**: GTP → dihydroneopterin triphosphate → carboxytetrahydropterin (CPH4) → carboxydeazaguanine (CDG) → free preQ0. The original pathway explicitly lists and orders GTP-CYCLOHYDRO-I-RXN, RXN0-5507, RXN0-6575 and RXN-12093, and includes MG1655. The fresh QuickGO definition exactly denotes formation of preQ0. This is a curator-grounded correspondence between independently inspected definitions; no native GO cross-reference is invented.

Both the working tree and original Documents checkout were searched with hidden and ignored files included, excluding only Git objects and dependency environments. The pre-author search found inventories and a prior research pause, not a maintained PreQ0 record. The final search adds exact reaction, protein and publication identifiers; its output is external, and new candidate files are explicitly distinguished from the baseline. The prior `propanoyl-assessment.json` says PreQ0/queuosine allocation was paused after the methylcitrate discovery, with no second pathway authored. The existing FolE step in folate/pterin records does not cover QueD, QueE, QueC or this endpoint. The full command receipts and prior dispositions are in `preq0-dedup.json`.

## Source boundaries and defects

Their complete subject inventories, all reaction side objects, catalysts and dispositions are preserved in `preq0-source-projection.json`; raw complete members and the all-object projection remain external with hashes. Reaction 5180 preserves obsolete one-ATP → ADP/phosphate/water accounting. Reaction 5181 is the downstream QueF reduction to preQ1, outside the exact native endpoint. It neither proves an E. coli pathway nor resolves those catalyst identities.

QueF reduction, Tgt insertion, QueA/QueG/QueH maturation, transport, archaeosine and antibiotic branches remain separate processes. The retained native PWY-6700 definition and the inspected YhhQ paper establish context; they do not justify importing their entire broader scope. The selected native pathway has not been shortened to evade those source defects: all four members of the independently defined PWY-6703 route are represented.

## Chemistry and current-source differences

Every substrate/product edge comes from the original **Rhea 142 full RDF**, including explicit directionalReaction, substrates/products, side `containsN`, participant `compound` and compound `accession` links. The 22 side entries for RHEA:17474, 27967, 27975 and 27983 retain exact coefficients, charge forms and source IDs. All four equations balance by atoms and charge using independent ChEBI 255 formula/charge statements.

Current RHEA:27983 consumes **two ATP** and produces **two AMP plus two diphosphate**. Both MetaCyc 22.5 RXN-12093 and the PathBank map retain an obsolete one-ATP/ADP equation, despite the native MetaCyc cross-reference pointing to the same Rhea identifier. The pinned Rhea 139 Semantic SQL label is likewise obsolete. The record uses the independently inspected current Rhea 142 equation and label; root integration supplies an explicit original-RDF authority route, without weakening label policy or silently changing the source version. The older label is not biological evidence. Current UniProt P77756 independently agrees with the current net equation and assigns it by HAMAP inference.

The four current QueC component reactions 85175, 85179, 85183 and 85187 describe two adenylations through an amide intermediate. The complete net transformation is selected once; the component reactions are not additionally asserted, avoiding duplicate substrate/product accounting. Full PMID:26228534 establishes this mechanism experimentally for **Streptomyces rimosus ToyM**, not purified E. coli QueC. Its original BioC passages 5–9 explicitly describe ATP dependence, AMP, pyrophosphate, the amide and adenylated intermediate. A magnesium assay effect in that homolog does not justify an E. coli QueC magnesium-cofactor edge.

QueE's selected net equation has no SAM consumption. Full PMID:23194065 shows SAM regeneration over multiple turnovers in **Bacillus subtilis QueE**. E. coli FldA/FPR used to activate that Bacillus enzyme are assay components, not established physiological electron donors for the E. coli enzyme. No donor edge, stoichiometric SAM-cleavage output or free flavin substitute is fabricated.

## Protein, assembly, cofactor and organism scope

Fresh complete reviewed UniProt entries from release **2026_03** identify FolE **P0A6T5**, QueD **P65870**, QueE **P64554** and QueC **P77756** as K-12 proteins. Their exact source pointers and evidence codes are attached to the narrow graph edges.

- **FolE:** MetaCyc FOLE-CPLX explicitly contains ten GTP-CYCLOHYDRO-I-MONOMER units; the current UniProt cross-reference connects the monomer to P0A6T5. Native and reviewed assembly evidence supports the homodecamer. Zinc-binding features are retained; native residue numbering differs by one from current UniProt and is not silently normalized. Direct E. coli FolE activity is also inspected in the complete 2009 QueD manuscript.
- **QueD:** MetaCyc CPLX0-8123 contains six G7431-MONOMER units, and the monomer has an explicit P65870 unification cross-reference. The selected complex corresponds to the crystallographic hexamer. Current UniProt separately reports trimers and dodecamers in solution under PMID:24816091 and a crystallographic hexamer under PMID:24990950. The difference remains explicit; the sources do not establish one universally applicable state. Exact E. coli CPH4 formation is directly demonstrated in full PMID:19231875, including isotope-resolved mass spectra, co-elution and an approximately one-zinc-per-subunit measurement. Alternative sepiapterin and pyruvoyltetrahydropterin activities are outside this native route.
- **QueE:** E. coli catalytic activity, homodimer and cluster/SAM/Mg assignments carry **ECO:0000255 / HAMAP MF_00917**. The graph uses a qualified gene-product `enables` role and preserves the inferred dimer in prose; it does not assert monomer sufficiency. No independently issued E. coli assembly identity was verified. Full PMID:19354300 and PMID:23194065 characterize the Bacillus protein; expression in E. coli is not an E. coli enzyme experiment. The original 2009 manuscript has an inconsistent E. coli heading in one assay section, but its cloning, expression, purification, results and figure identify Bacillus QueE; the isolated heading is not used to reassign the protein.
- **QueC:** Exact E. coli net chemistry and zinc binding carry **ECO:0000255 / HAMAP MF_01633**. The qualified `enables` edge does not assert a resolved assembly or monomer sufficiency. PMID:16199558's inspected abstract provides E. coli B105 complementation and placement upstream of preQ0; it does not measure the exact purified-enzyme stoichiometry. Bacillus QueC and Streptomyces ToyM data retain their original taxa.

The original mixed-species reconstruction uses E. coli FolE/QueD, Bacillus QueE and Bacillus QueC or Streptomyces ToyM. It is strong evidence for the complete biochemical route, but not a four-enzyme E. coli purification experiment. Native MG1655 membership and the current K-12 gene-product annotations support the curated organism scope with these limits. Anaerobic in vitro handling protects CPH4 and the radical-SAM cluster; it does not establish that cellular PreQ0 synthesis is restricted to anaerobic growth.
