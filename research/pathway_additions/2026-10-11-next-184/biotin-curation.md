The 2010 E. coli study supports the complete precursor route by engineered growth bypass, cell-extract reconstruction, BioH assays and FAS inhibitors. It does not establish purified-substrate kinetics for every individual FAS assignment. Native FabG/FabZ/FabI/FabB links carry indirect `EV-EXP-IMP` evidence. Current UniProt FUNCTION prose for FabG/FabZ/FabB is not falsely described as a biotin-specific experiment. [The protein projection](biotin-protein-projection.json) retains selected original annotation comments and all associated ECO codes.

The first condensation, RXN-11474, remains present with complete chemistry and no catalyst edge. The 2012 BioC paper explains why an ACP substrate excludes the older FabH proposal; it considers redundant FabB/F possibilities without establishing a unique initiating enzyme. Individual knockout survival and the lethal double deletion do not prove purified-substrate sufficiency. FabF was investigated but is not a declared catalyst.

E. coli BioC is supported by genetics and weak activity in refolded/coupled preparations. The direct purified 2012 malonyl-ACP methyltransferase experiments used **Bacillus cereus** BioC that complements an E. coli mutant. The later 2024 study used **Acinetobacter baumannii** BioC and still describes E. coli purification difficulty. Neither the foreign enzyme's oligomer nor its purified kinetics is reassigned to E. coli. The exact current E. coli accession remains P12999.

BioH's physiological ACP substrate is supported by the 2012 BioH S82A substrate complex, mutagenesis, hydrolysis and E. coli complementation. This trapped enzyme-substrate complex is not a permanent assembly. The older current-UniProt suggestion of BioH making pimeloyl-CoA is excluded. BioF's 2010 ACP-versus-CoA acceptance is a growth-bypass inference, whereas the inspected older kinetic abstract directly studies pimeloyl-CoA. The record does not claim a purified BioF ACP experiment from that paragraph.

Eight native assemblies are retained: BioF/BioA/BioD/BioB dimers, FabG/FabI tetramers, FabB dimer and FabZ hexamer. Each native monomer is independently bridged to its exact E. coli UniProt accession.
