# Molybdenum cofactor biosynthesis: exact route and source disposition

The new record is **MetaCyc:PWY-6823, molybdenum cofactor biosynthesis**, at `data/pathways/molybdenum-cofactor-biosynthesis.yaml`. It represents all eight reactions in the inspected MetaCyc 22.5 pathway, including cysteine desulfuration, MoaD activation and sulfur recycling, and ends at unmodified Mo-molybdopterin. The selected protein core is **Escherichia coli K-12 (NCBITaxon:83333)**. Native assemblies are curated for MG1655, within that scope; individual experimental strains and assay readouts are narrower. This is a reaction-level pathway, not a claim that every transient enzyme intermediate or physiological electron donor is known.

The initial PathBank lead **SMP0001902 / PW001888.owl** is not an exact equivalent. Its ten document reactions combine five folate/pterin reactions, incomplete MoaA chemistry, MPT synthesis, adenylation, molybdate insertion, and downstream MocA cytidylation. The prior thiamine scout correctly deferred it. This follow-up resolves a separately defined complete native route; it does **not** declare all of that PathBank map covered. In particular, the controller gap at reaction 3398 belongs to the adjacent folate branch, and Q46810 is the experimentally characterized MocA cytidylyltransferase rather than merely an obsolete uncharacterized protein. The MCD branch remains a distinct follow-up. GO:0006777 covers additional cofactor products with one or two MPT ligands, and GO:0032324 also includes tungsten; neither is substituted for this exact boundary.

## Identity, direction and chemistry

The native reaction set is RXN-8340, RXN-17809, RXN0-308, RXN-11361, RXN-12473, RXN-8342, RXN-8344 and RXN-8348. It maps to current Rhea directional reactions **49577, 49581, 17458, 43617, 48613, 26334, 31332 and 35048**, respectively. The full RDF replay retains all **44 chemical sides**, their actual `rh:containsN` coefficients, and all **eight balanced reactions**.

RXN-8340 has an obsolete RHEA:26378 cross-reference to the combined GTP-to-cPMP transformation. Its actual cyclic-GTP product CPD-19179, independently linked to CHEBI:131766, its EC 4.1.99.22, its native MoaA enzyme assignment, and current reviewed P30745/RHEA:49576 identify the distinct cyclase step. MoaC completes the conversion in RHEA:49581. The incorrect old cross-reference is preserved and rejected explicitly; no master/directed family equivalence is fabricated.

PathBank 5153 uses the same thiocarboxylated carrier on both sides and has inconsistent carrier counts. Current Rhea instead consumes two thiocarboxylated MoaD equivalents and regenerates two carboxy-MoaD equivalents. The native RXN-8342 releases four protons, whereas current Rhea releases two for its explicit chemistry. Native RXN-8348 consumes a proton and produces water; current Rhea instead releases a proton and describes Mo(VI)O2(OH)-MPT. The old CPD-8123 CML is MoO2-MPT, C10H10MoN5O8PS2, charge −2, even though it carries the same CHEBI:71302 cross-reference. Current ChEBI/Rhea describes C10H11MoN5O9PS2, charge −4. These formal chemical representations are not treated as identical, nor as direct measurements of the intracellular redox/ligand state. Current balanced Rhea chemistry is the selected graph representation.

The five RHEA-COMP identifiers retain whole IscS and MoaD carrier states. Formula checks use their native `reactivePart` formula to cancel the unchanged protein backbone, without replacing the graph nodes by isolated residues. Both formal AH2/A redox pairs remain generic. Dithionite or DTT used in reconstitution is not declared the physiological electron donor. The net sulfur-transfer reaction does not duplicate its component transformations as a second route.

## Enzymes, assemblies and evidence strength

Nine reviewed K-12 proteins are included: MoaA P30745, MoaC P0A738, MoaD P30748, MoaE P30749, MoeA P12281, MoeB P12282, Mog P0AF03, IscS P0A6B7 and YnjE P78067. P30747 is a demerged accession; its organism-specific successors are not interchangeable. The K-12 MoaC entry is P0A738.

The six independently identified native assemblies are the MoaC hexamer (CPLX-8331), MoaD2-MoaE2 synthase (CPLX0-2502), MoeB homodimer (CPLX0-8164), Mog homotrimer (CPLX0-8163), MoeA homodimer (CPLX0-8045), and IscS homodimer (CPLX0-248). Every component coefficient and native monomer-to-UniProt cross-reference is replayed. The Thermus complex CPLX-8334 is excluded. A transient MoeB2-MoaD2 complex is described as a substrate-associated catalytic state, not invented as an additional externally identified species. Current MoaA annotation reports monomer/homodimer; its functional `enables` edge does not claim isolated-monomer sufficiency.

The full primary papers PMID:23627491 and PMID:25941396 explicitly distinguish **Staphylococcus aureus MoaA** from **E. coli MoaC**. The latter directly supports E. coli kinetics, MoaC structure and complementation (Figure 1E–G; Figure 2A; Materials and Methods). K-12 MoaA cyclase and two-cluster assignments remain HAMAP ECO:0000255 in the record. Expression of a foreign enzyme in E. coli is not direct evidence for the K-12 enzyme. Anaerobic nitrate respiration was the MoaC complementation readout; oxygen-sensitive radical-SAM handling does not prove the whole pathway operates only anaerobically.

Full PMID:19946146 supports IscS-specific interaction and sulfur delivery in the tested E. coli system, while also documenting in vitro donation by CsdA and SufS and residual activity in deletion strains. PMID:21856748's inspected primary abstract supports **YnjE enhancement** in a defined IscS/MoeB/MoaD reconstitution. It does not demonstrate isolated YnjE catalysis of the complete net reaction or universal in vivo necessity. YnjE's separate thiosulfate reaction is not mistaken for this role. Its periplasmic annotation and the uncertain cellular relay prevent a blanket compartment assertion. TusA's pleiotropic, condition-dependent effects in PMID:23281480 are retained as context rather than a chemically resolved direct MoaD-transfer edge.

PMID:12571226 supports the two-MoaD-per-cPMP stoichiometry and MoaD/MoaE synthase architecture. Current UniProt directly cites experimental evidence for MoeB adenylation, Mog adenylation and MoeA insertion. PMID:15632135 used purified E. coli proteins and human apo-sulfite oxidase as a readout; the latter is not an E. coli pathway catalyst. Its thiol inhibition differs from older extract-dependent assays, so no glutathione-consumption or obligatory glutathione-cofactor edge is added. Tungsten substitution and high-molybdate bypasses remain condition-specific context.

Cofactor edges retain source-specific support: inferred MoaA [4Fe-4S] clusters; experimentally annotated MoeB zinc, MoeA magnesium and IscS PLP. Magnesium supplied with ATP does not establish a separate bound-magnesium cofactor for MoeB or Mog. No sulfur-bearing MoeB intermediate is invented.
