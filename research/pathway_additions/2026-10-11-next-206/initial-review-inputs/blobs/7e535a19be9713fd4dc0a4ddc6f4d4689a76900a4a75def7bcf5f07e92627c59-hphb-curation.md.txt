# Complete archaeal 3HP/4HB cycle

This record implements MetaCyc:PWY-5789 in Metallosphaera sedula TH2 (DSM 5348; NCBITaxon:399549). The issuing authority calls the pathway “3-hydroxypropanoate/4-hydroxybutanate cycle”; its spelling is retained as the canonical label. GO:0043427 is not an exact mapping: that term describes the Chloroflexus cycle producing glyoxylate by malyl-CoA cleavage. The archaeal pathway instead regenerates acetyl-CoA through succinyl-CoA reduction, 4-hydroxybutyryl-CoA dehydration and thiolysis. The two pathways share reactions without having the same complete boundary.

The initial source assessment and fresh searches included hidden and ignored files in both the original checkout and the owned worktree, excluding Git internals and virtual environments only. Exact native identity, synonyms, defining reactions, protein accessions and publications found previous carbon-fixation leads and shared chemistry, but no complete maintained HP/HB record. The deduplication receipt records the actual commands, roots, dates and output hashes. Earlier research was read, including the 2026-10-05 cross-Mech protein report and the next-203 carbon-fixation access follow-up; those are leads, not evidence for an absence claim beyond the searched corpus.

## Boundary and chemistry

All 18 original native reaction objects are accounted for. There are 16 biological enzyme transformations and one auxiliary CO2 hydration. Native BIOTIN-CARBOXYL-RXN and RXN0-5055 describe the two halves of acetyl-CoA carboxylation. Adding their original charged equations cancels exactly the same carboxylated and uncarboxylated biotin-carrier states, leaving independently issued RHEA:11309. No proton, water or carbon term was silently dropped. The resulting graph has 17 reaction nodes, without also retaining the same half-reactions as separate turnovers.

This composition is biologically necessary for the chosen archaeon: the source half-reactions name bacterial BCCP-dimer classes, whereas the original M. sedula experiments identify a three-subunit holoenzyme with a proposed tetramer-of-heterotrimers organization. The graph preserves that physical mechanism with complex/subunit relations and bound biotin, without treating free biotin as a net substrate or asserting a bacterial carrier assembly in the archaeon.

The original native equations, in each pathway-layout direction, match the selected Rhea equations after the declared native-to-ChEBI mappings. All 78 Rhea side entries and coefficients are retained, including protons, water, phosphate, pyrophosphate, CoA and pyridine nucleotides. Seventeen atom/charge balances are checked independently against ChEBI 255. The terminal thiolase produces two acetyl-CoA molecules, one of which regenerates the acceptor. With the auxiliary hydration represented once, the formal sum consumes one CO2 and one bicarbonate; representing hydration twice would express both carbon inputs as CO2. This convention is not a measured partition of carbon uptake.

Twenty-nine native chemical structures match the selected ChEBI InChI exactly. Two source details need explicit disposition. Native PPI cites CHEBI:29888, while its actual InChI is the triply charged species CHEBI:33019 selected by current Rhea. Native CROTONYL-COA leaves one conserved CoA hydroxyl stereocenter unspecified; current CHEBI:57332 specifies it. The trans acyl-double-bond geometry agrees. The latter is a broader-to-specific structure mapping, not exactMatch, and is qualified on both affected pathway membership edges. The native summary's repeated 4-hydroxybutyryl-CoA intermediate is not followed: the original reaction objects and primary stereochemical assays require (S)-3-hydroxybutyryl-CoA after crotonyl-CoA hydration.

The complete primary isotope study (PMID:21169486) used autotrophic, microaerobic TH2 at 75 degrees C and pH 2. It supports the cycle and a major succinyl-CoA biosynthetic exit. The graph therefore does not present acetyl-CoA as the exclusive physiological exit or claim a fixed flux through every depicted alternative enzyme.

## Protein identity, assembly and experimental scope

The original 2003 carboxylase genes provide a decisive independent correction. AF461115/AAO15571.1 encodes the carboxyltransferase and exactly matches A4YGI1. AF461116/AAO15572.1 encodes biotin carboxylase and exactly matches A4YD22; AAO15573.1 encodes its biotin carrier and exactly matches A4YD23. Native MONOMER-13716 and MONOMER-13717 reverse the last two UniProt cross-references. Both original assertions are retained in the research projection, and the graph follows the original CDS products, full translation equality and primary N-terminal sequences. It does not manufacture a new complex identity. The observed native mass and subunit ratio support the proposed (alpha-beta-gamma)4 architecture; they are not a high-resolution assembly determination.

Eight independently identified native complexes are retained. The malonyl/succinyl-CoA reductase tetramer is a curated assembly supported by approximate mass of a partially purified M. sedula preparation. A separate S. tokodaii recombinant preparation bound RNA, so its mass is not treated as an unambiguous tetramer measurement. The Msed_1993 reductase, 3-hydroxypropionyl-CoA synthetase, epimerase and Msed_1424 reductase retain the experimentally supported trimer, tetramer, dimer and dimer interpretations with their original methods. The 2017 study (PMID:27771364) reports an octameric recombinant Msed_2001 preparation and pentameric Msed_1424 preparation, contrasting with the earlier monomer and dimer estimates. These differences remain unresolved. It also supports a recombinant (HPCS)4(HPCD)8 association and a positive yeast two-hybrid interaction, but no corresponding external whole-assembly identity was verified. The observation is retained on the activity edge rather than merged into the native HPCS homotetramer identity. ACR/SSR coexpression improved solubility without a positive two-hybrid interaction. The same paper inconsistently names its MCR construct Msed_0709 in Methods and Msed_0745 in Results, while citing the earlier S. tokodaii protocol; its MCR rate is therefore not promoted to unqualified selected-construct evidence.

The mutase's active alpha2beta2 assembly is directly supported by coexpression and size-exclusion results in PMID:22752162. Native CPLX-8531/8532 have ambiguous nested dimer coefficients; CPLX-8531's HTML title is only “cplx-8531.” Neither nested object is promoted to a separate graph component. The record instead connects the verified CPLX-7576 identity to two copies of each original Msed_0638/Msed_2055 protein, as established by the primary work. Reverse mutase activity was higher in the assay; the selected forward direction follows the experimentally supported autotrophic route.

Msed_1424 is particularly important: its directly characterized NADPH-dependent succinic semialdehyde reductase activity and native CPLX-7948 disagree with the current UniProt acetylating-dehydrogenase annotation. The latter is retained as a conflict and is not used as catalytic evidence. Similarly, native ENZRXN-14671 points to the S. tokodaii acryloyl-CoA reductase. Native M. sedula peptide identification (100% identity, 73% coverage), its complete sequence entry and the original primary distinction support Msed_1426 in this record; the 2009 detailed recombinant kinetic and zinc/monomer observations remain explicitly cross-species or similarity-based. The later full primary PMID:27771364 directly measures recombinant Msed_1426 after coexpression with Msed_1424 and separation by gel filtration; that stronger activity evidence is included.

Later full primary studies change the old terminal-module interpretation. PMID:32218776 supports Msed_1423 as the principal hydroxybutyryl-CoA dehydrogenase. PMID:33472982 supports Msed_2001 as the principal dehydratase/crotonase, with about 99%/86% capacity estimated from measured kinetics and protein abundance. Those percentages are not knockout or flux measurements. The measured alternatives Msed_0399, Msed_0336, Msed_0384 and Msed_0389 remain qualified activities, not equally supported obligatory cycle enzymes. Msed_0394 is a measured alternative 4-hydroxybutyrate ligase, less strongly supported as a major physiological contributor than Msed_0406. The engineered Msed_1353 W424G activity and unsupported historical Msed_1422/1220/0566 assignments are excluded from the cycle graph with their reasons retained in the protein projection.

Protein `enables` edges assert functional gene-product roles without inventing monomer sufficiency or a physical assembly identifier. Msed_1321's approximate tetramer is described as such, based on its measured mass. E. coli was an expression host in several experiments; it is not the pathway taxon. Other native listed taxa, including the organism using the distinct dicarboxylate/4-hydroxybutyrate cycle, are not inherited indiscriminately.

## Cofactors and limits

Biotin is a covalently bound carrier prosthetic group. Direct carboxylase assays show a divalent-ion dependence: omission left less than 1% activity, magnesium restored the MgATP-dependent assay, and other ions partly substituted. The magnesium relation does not claim a permanently bound ion stoichiometry. The epimerase's Ni/Co/Mg edges similarly preserve experimentally tested alternatives, without selecting an established intracellular metal.

The Msed_1424 preparation contained 1.9 zinc atoms per monomer, while EDTA did not inhibit it; adding zinc did not activate the already loaded preparation. Acryloyl-CoA reductase zinc and the two ligases' Mg/Mn annotations retain ECO:0000250 similarity qualifications. Adenosylcobalamin is assigned to the mutase complex, not represented as free cobalt. The Msed_1321 FAD and iron-sulfur assignments are explicit native database assertions. The 2007 discovery paper was not inspected in full, and the later activity experiment is not substituted for direct cofactor-occupancy measurement. The native cluster identifier is not an experimentally determined oxidation state.

Four measured carboxylase product-inhibition relations retain the original assay concentrations and residual activities. They do not assert intracellular regulatory effects. No unverified location or transporter is added. PMID:25939834 directly tests HhcR binding in M. yellowstonensis; the M. sedula regulon is a comparative-genomic inference reconciled with omics. This contextual lead is retained without transferring the other species' binding experiment to Msed_2175. The newer Rhea-linked PMID:39019246 concerns the distinct Chloroflexus reductase domain and is not evidence for the archaeal enzyme. The root-managed iModulonDB inventory has no Metallosphaera dataset; its applicability receipt is context only.
