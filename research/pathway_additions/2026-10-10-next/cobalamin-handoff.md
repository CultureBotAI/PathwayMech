## Organism-specific evidence and limits

Current reviewed K-12 accessions are BtuR **P0A9H5**, CobU **P0AE76**, CobT **P36562**, CobS **P36561** and CobC **P52086** (UniProt release 2026_03). BtuR's catalytic cross-references describe cobyrinate and complete cobalamin, not the exact cobinamide reaction found separately in Rhea. CobU catalysis and homodimer, CobT homodimer, CobS functional annotation/Mg assignment and CobC catalytic assignment carry similarity qualifiers. Absence of an inline qualifier on a reaction comment does not turn it into primary experimental evidence. E. coli MetaCyc RIBAZOLEPHOSPHAT-MONOMER explicitly describes an uncharacterized protein predicted from Salmonella CobC. MetaCyc CPLX-342 is Pseudomonas CobP and CPLX-7417 is Salmonella CobT: neither is an E. coli assembly identifier. [Reviewed CobC](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P52086/entry).

**PMID7592411**, full 1995 PDF, experimentally supports E. coli K-12 salvage through growth genetics. Tables 2/3 (printed page 6375; PDF page 5, visually inspected) distinguish cobS, cobT and btuR mutants, supplemented DMB and oxygen conditions. CobS loss prevents rescue by cobinamide even with DMB; CobT loss can be rescued by DMB. BtuR loss blocks the tested aerobic route but permits anaerobic cobinamide use. Therefore BtuR cannot be called universally essential under all conditions. The substrate was dicyanocobinamide and the readout used methionine auxotrophy; this is not a purified assay for every current charged Rhea species. The cloning source is W3110, not proof for every MG1655-specific claim. Historical interpretation of CobT as supporting DMB synthesis must not override its current phosphoribosyltransferase assignment. [Full primary PDF](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC177486?pdf=render).

**PMID10518530** directly reconstitutes nucleotide-loop assembly using **Salmonella typhimurium** enzymes. Results and methods identify the phosphorylated final intermediate and CobC dephosphorylation; E. coli BL21 used to express a construct is not evidence that the enzyme itself is E. coli. **PMID17209023**, inspected through its primary abstract, reassesses terminal CobC dephosphorylation specifically in **Salmonella enterica**. These support pathway chemistry and a homology rationale, not direct E. coli CobC characterization. [1999 primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC18366/), [2007 primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17209023/).

**PMID34031037** includes an EcCobS-containing membrane fraction as a salvage control in a BL21-derived host. Its assay uses heterologous accessory enzymes, including Vibrio CobC; it does not directly establish E. coli CobC catalysis. The EcCobS construct's exact bridge to P36561 needs verification. MgCl2 in a reaction mixture is not independent proof of enzyme-bound Mg dependence. Corrinoid extraction includes cyanation, limiting direct inference about the native upper ligand from the final LC-MS peak. Vibrio direct remodeling and phosphatase phenotypes must not be transferred to E. coli. [2021 primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8407341/).
