# Complete dTDP-L-rhamnose biosynthesis

The record represents the complete four-step native MetaCyc `DTDPRHAMSYN-PWY` route, using the exact, non-obsolete process identity `GO:0019305` (dTDP-rhamnose biosynthetic process). It starts with alpha-D-glucose 1-phosphate and dTTP and ends with dTDP-beta-L-rhamnose. It does not append antigen assembly, transport, free-rhamnose metabolism, UDP-rhamnose or GDP-rhamnose biosynthesis. The record is `data/pathways/dtdp-l-rhamnose-biosynthesis.yaml`.

The final native reaction is written in its oxidative direction and marked reversible; the pathway layout selects reduction. The first native Rhea cross-reference is **bidirectional** RHEA:15228, not the master. Its explicit `rh:bidirectionalReaction` link from master 15225 and `rh:directionalReaction` link to 15226 are both retained. The remaining three native cross-references are their masters. All selected directions and 15 chemical side entries are replayed from the complete pinned Rhea 142 RDF, rather than inferred from a displayed equation. Every coefficient is one. All four reactions independently balance elements and charge against ChEBI 255 and native Rhea formulas.

The endpoint's canonical ChEBI name is `dTDP-6-deoxy-beta-L-mannose(2-)` (CHEBI:57510); the issuing authority supplies `dTDP-beta-L-rhamnose` as a synonym. This is the same beta-L nucleotide sugar, not an alpha sugar or free L-rhamnose. The names of the two 4-keto intermediates preserve the exact current stereochemical forms. The complete net consumes one dTTP, one alpha-D-glucose 1-phosphate, one NADPH and two hydrons, producing one dTDP-beta-L-rhamnose, one diphosphate, one water and one NADP. No pyrophosphatase reaction is added outside the native boundary.

## Protein, assembly and evidence scope

All four newly retrieved reviewed UniProt entries are release 2026_03, taxon 83333 (E. coli K-12). Their canonical names, full entry digests, entry metadata and exact comment indices are retained. Gene order is not confused with pathway order: P37744 is rfbA/rmlA (b2039), P37759 is rfbB/rmlB (b2041), P37745 is rfbC/rmlC (b2038), and P37760 is rfbD/rmlD (b2040).

* **RmlA:** PMID:11697907 directly studies E. coli enzyme kinetics and its homotetramer. Only the complete indexed abstract was available; the publisher returned a redirect shell, not full article text. The RCSB 1H5R primary entity independently identifies E. coli as the protein source and links P37744 through SIFTS. MetaCyc CPLX0-8034 contains four copies of DTDPGLUCOSEPP-MONOMER, explicitly unified with P37744. Magnesium binding, one per subunit, is attributed specifically to the reviewed ECO:0000269 UniProt annotation, not to uninspected pages or to MgATP assay composition.
* **RmlB:** MetaCyc DTDPGLUCDEHYDRAT-CPLX contains two copies of DTDPGLUCDEHYDRAT-MONOMER, explicitly unified with P37759. Current exact catalysis, NAD binding and dimerization use ECO:0000250 from P27830, which is the E. coli K-12 **RffG paralog**. They retain that inference tier. The native 88-kDa experimental citation is PMID:4307450, explicitly E. coli B. It is not direct K-12 RmlB evidence. The native monomer's PMID:11601973 mechanism citation and the inspected Hegeman 2002 primary PDF concern RffG, not a new direct RmlB experiment. The bound NAD cycles internally; it is not a net reaction substrate.
* **RmlC:** Exact catalysis and homodimer annotation in P37745 use ECO:0000250 from Salmonella P26394. The complete PMID:10217500 primary paper adds independent K-12 mutant/extract support: all its E. coli strains are K-12 derivatives (Table 1, p.664); the rmlC deletion loses the free 4-keto-rhamnose intermediate and dTDP-rhamnose production. Purified-enzyme, deuteration and equilibrium measurements in that paper use **M. tuberculosis RmlC** and are not presented as purified K-12 measurements. The functional `enables` edge does not claim monomeric sufficiency.
* **RmlD:** P37760 explicitly labels pathway involvement Probable (ECO:0000305, PMID:7517391). Catalysis, Mg binding, homodimerization and near-equal NADH/NADPH use are ECO:0000250 from Salmonella P26392. PMID:12057193 explicitly identifies its purified structural enzyme as Salmonella. These claims remain inferred for K-12. The graph retains the full native NADPH route and does not claim that NADH cannot support reduction. A functional `enables` edge and a qualified per-monomer magnesium-binding edge do not establish an independently sufficient monomer. The attempted META DTDPDEHYRHAMREDUCT-MONOMER retrieval returned 404; its EcoCyc cross-reference is not silently converted into a verified MetaCyc assembly identity.

## Resolved source conflicts and exclusions

The MetaCyc RmlC monomer comment says that epimerase and reductase form a joint synthetase complex and that the intermediate cannot be released. Its own cited PMID:10217500 demonstrates a **free** intermediate; the full primary Discussion (p.669) says the epimerase can function independently of the reductase, while leaving possible in vivo interaction open. The source-to-graph ledger explicitly rejects obligatory channeling/complex membership. The graph retains both reactions and their shared free intermediate. It neither deletes the epimerase step nor invents a RmlC/RmlD complex.

The native pathway expressly excludes DTDPGLUCOSEPP2-MONOMER and DTDPGLUCDEHYDRAT2-MONOMER. These are the RffH/RffG alternatives involved in enterobacterial common-antigen precursor synthesis. PMID:7559340 does demonstrate RffG compensation for an rfbB defect and RffH/RffG biochemical activities. This alternative is not asserted absent, but it is outside the selected native core. The full 1995 PDF was read: its rfbBDAC donor is O7 strain VW187 even when expressed in a K-12 host, and its historical rfbC/rfbD terminology is inconsistent with modern accession labels. No phenotype or exact enzyme identity is transferred merely because the expression host is E. coli.

The complete PMID:7517391 PDF identifies the K-12 rhamnose genes by similarity and distinguishes defective laboratory lineages from reconstructed O16-producing strains. PMID:10217500 uses repaired-rfb BW24599 and derivatives. These are not measurements of constitutive O-antigen production by all K-12 strains. The record's taxon and narrative preserve that limit, and no downstream O-antigen production edge is added. In particular, the native pathway's general mentions of ECA and other species do not enlarge the four-step rfb pathway.

No cellular compartment, physical RmlC/D heterocomplex, regulation, generic growth phenotype, or new local molecular identifier was inferred from absence of evidence. Native assemblies were independently verified from their component crosswalks and canonical META HTML, not manufactured from oligomer count alone.
