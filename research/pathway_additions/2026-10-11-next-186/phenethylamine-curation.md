# Phenylethylamine degradation I

The record represents the complete two-reaction native **MetaCyc:2PHENDEG-PWY** boundary, from phenylethylamine through phenylacetaldehyde to phenylacetate. Both reactions and their complete current Rhea equations are retained. The PathBank inventory lead is broader; neither its full diagram nor the alternative quinohemoprotein route is claimed as represented here.

Primary K-12 enzyme and mutant abstracts (PMID:3309152 and PMID:9043126) support the route and enzyme activity. Complete primary studies PMID:24013633 and PMID:23913322 provide MG1655 transcription and W3110 growth/peroxide context, respectively. Abstract-only inspection is distinguished from full article inspection in the manifest. Current full UniProt entries identify TynA, FeaB and FeaR; native XML resolves pathway membership and the TynA homodimer. Exact source bodies are hashed and retained externally.

Rhea142 specifies protonated 2-phenylethanaminium CHEBI:225237, rather than neutral phenylethylamine, and two product protons in FeaB oxidation. Both equations are replayed against their native RDF participants and independent ChEBI formulas/charges. The native PHENDEHYD-RXN is reversible; the chosen directed Rhea form follows the native catabolic pathway layout and does not assert thermodynamic irreversibility.

FeaB is represented by a supported gene-product/activity association. The native tetramer and current UniProt dimer assignments conflict. The 1997 PadA study concerns E. coli W; the 2006 tetramer experiment used a supplied PadA construct whose exact equivalence to K-12 P80668 was not established. No fixed assembly or active isolated monomer is asserted. This uncertainty does not remove either native reaction or its catalyst association. NAD+ is the selected native route, without excluding reported NADP+ activity.

The graph preserves experimentally annotated TynA copper, calcium and covalently bound TPQ; similarity-only zinc/manganese claims are not promoted to established K-12 cofactors. Periplasmic TynA and cytosolic FeaB are explicit. Their shared aldehyde is represented without inventing transport machinery. FeaR has a conditional transcriptional regulation edge, without inferring direct aldehyde binding or enzyme allostery. Predicted CRP/NAC promoter motifs and downstream phenylacetate assimilation are outside the included assertions.
