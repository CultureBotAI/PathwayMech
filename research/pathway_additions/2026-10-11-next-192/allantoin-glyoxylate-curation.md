This is the complete four-reaction **MetaCyc:PWY-5705** route in *Escherichia coli* K-12. The original assigned PathBank lead, SMP0002064/PW002050, is already represented by the full anaerobic oxamate record, MetaCyc:PWY0-41. That duplicate is recorded in `allantoin-duplicate-disposition.json`. The parent explicitly authorized PWY-5705 as a separate, exact pathway task after this finding; it is not a partial replacement for the oxamate pathway.

The maintained target is `data/pathways/allantoin-degradation-to-glyoxylate-iii.yaml`. It contains 21 participants, four reactions, 41 edges, 80 evidence assertions and 23 references. Its 27 named nodes include the pathway and taxon. The byte hash and actual checks are in the validation receipt.

## Identity, full boundary and novelty

The original MetaCyc 22.5 XML defines PWY-5705 as the PWY-5698 subpathway followed by UREIDOGLYCOLATE-LYASE-RXN. PWY-5698 has exactly ALLANTOINASE-RXN, ALLANTOATE-DEIMINASE-RXN and URUR-RXN. The source orders all four in the degradative direction, ending at glyoxylate and urea. Independently fetched native HTML exposes the exact META object identity and canonical title; the production authority parser resolves it without a label exception. GO:0000256 is broader. GO:0009442 is obsolete in the pinned GO database and includes both glyoxylate and oxamate branches, so neither is an exact pathway identifier.

Ignored- and hidden-inclusive searches covered the current worktree and original Documents checkout, excluding only Git internals, virtual environments and Python bytecode caches. Exact IDs, reaction frames, new AllA accession and gene aliases, publications, names and prior allantoin dispositions were inspected. The search receipts retain commands, scope, output hashes and matched paths. The original checkout was readable at base 0919419; no whole-computer absence claim is made.

The existing yeast PWY-5694 record is a three-reaction route: DAL2 allantoicase directly produces ureidoglycolate and urea from allantoate. This new E. coli variant instead uses **AllC followed by AllE**, retaining S-ureidoglycine and releasing carbon dioxide and two ammonium before AllA releases urea. The distinction is biochemical, not merely taxonomic. Existing PWY0-41 shares the first three reactions but continues through AllD and AllFGH toward oxamate; its different branch is not duplicated here. Downstream glycerate metabolism, uptake and transcriptional control are outside the exact PWY-5705 boundary.

## Chemistry and source conflicts

The selected current Rhea 142 directions are 17030, 27486, 25242 and 11305. All 17 side entries and their coefficients are retained, including the **two hydrons** consumed by 27486. The full original RDF, its master-to-direction triples, participant-to-compound links, formula/charge fields, and direction TSV are retained externally. Three metabolite-handoff edges separately attribute native ordering and the current Rhea chemical bridge; native pathway XML is not said to contain ChEBI IDs it does not contain.

The native AllC reaction has the stale Rhea cross-reference **13594**. Its full native chemical sides agree with the current 27485 family, which is independently assigned by reviewed P77425. The record explicitly preserves this source-version conflict rather than treating 13594 as a current exact identifier. The older AllC structural abstract describes a combined ureidoglycolate product; PMID:20038185 identifies S-ureidoglycine by NMR and separates the AllE reaction. No duplicate spontaneous ureidoglycine hydrolysis is added.

**AllA releases urea, not two ammonia molecules.** The full primary paper PMID:24107613/PMC3793230 traces the historical misannotation and directly tests recombinant E. coli AllA. Its continuous assay detects ammonia only when urease is added. Figure 2C further demonstrates loss of activity after EDTA treatment and rescue only by nickel among tested divalent ions. Current Rhea 11304 and reviewed P77731 agree. The paper also cautions that glyoxylate is largely hydrated in solution and that water participation in the elementary mechanism was not experimentally settled. The record retains the balanced formal lyase equation without claiming a proven microscopic mechanism. The old PathBank “hydrolase” label is not used as functional evidence.

## Organisms, components, metals and locations

The taxon is **NCBITaxon:83333, E. coli K-12**, matching all four reviewed UniProt entries. Native assembly frames are annotated to MG1655, while the 1999 physiological work used K-12 derivatives including ECL1 and the 2013 AllA experiment used ASKA clone JW0493. Expression hosts, structural entries and genome annotation are not treated as proof that every experiment used MG1655.

Full primary PMID:10601204/PMC94204 shows anaerobic allantoin nitrogen use in ECL1 with xylose as the carbon source, and failure to grow using allantoin as the sole carbon source. Deleting allA abolished the measured ureidoglycolate-cleaving activity but did **not** abolish nitrogen-supported growth. The oxamate branch can carry that nitrogen flux; this route is not asserted to be the unique or required assimilation pathway.

AllB has the independently declared native **CPLX-64**, four G6281/P77671 subunits. Native membership, current protein cross-reference and purified-protein gel filtration/cross-linking in PMID:11092864 support the tetramer. AllC has **CPLX-7524**, two G6285/P77425 subunits; the source assembly and current experimentally supported homodimer annotation are retained. AllE/P75713 is represented as the enzyme with its **curator-inferred monomer** status stated explicitly.

AllA/P77731 has a homodimer annotation supported by the structural submission for PDB 1XSQ and HAMAP. The independently inspected RCSB assembly is author-and-PISA-defined, not a fresh solution oligomerization measurement. Native G6275-MONOMER and the bounded PathBank PW000827 search expose a protein controller and no independently declared whole AllA complex. The qualified **enables** edge retains AllA's supported gene-product role without inventing an assembly identity or asserting isolated-monomer sufficiency. The AllA nickel relation likewise does not invent metal occupancy per protomer.

AllB zinc, nickel, cobalt and manganese annotations are alternatives, not four simultaneous requirements. Full primary PMID:12486048 reports different metal occupancy, activity and stereoselectivity for different recombinant preparations, favors zinc physiologically, and finds cobalt substitution also permits slower R-allantoin turnover. This record is the S-allantoin route. AllC's older binuclear zinc structure and subsequent manganese activity/binding are kept separately qualified. AllE uses manganese with cobalt as an annotated alternative. No metal is inferred solely from an assay buffer. Only AllC and AllE receive cytoplasm edges, with the actual UniProt GO cross-reference and evidence tiers: AllC IEA/curator inference, AllE experimental annotation. Those locations are not automatically extended to other proteins.
