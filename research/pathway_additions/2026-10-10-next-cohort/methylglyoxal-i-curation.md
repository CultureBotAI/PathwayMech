## Assemblies, cofactors and organism limits

GloA is a homodimer, and its two-subunit composition is retained. Ni2+ activates the E. coli enzyme, whereas Zn2+ does not. The 1998 solution study reported one nickel per dimer; the 2000 structural study's current UniProt annotation reports one per subunit/two per dimer. Both statements are retained as differing measurements; a universal intracellular occupancy is not asserted. The structural abstract also reports active Co2+ and Cd2+ complexes. These experimental alternatives are described without declaring them obligatory in vivo cofactors.

GloB's current experimental annotation reports two Zn2+ ions per monomer, with Mn2+ and Co2+ substitution in reconstitution experiments. No Ni dependence is transferred from GloA. Dld's FAD is enzyme-bound, not consumed in the net reaction; primary structural support was inspected as an indexed abstract, not the full article. The current protein annotation places Dld on the cytoplasmic side of the inner membrane as a peripheral protein. The plasma-membrane node preserves this location without asserting integral topology or whole-pathway localization. The FAD charge state follows its current UniProt ChEBI grounding.

The native PathBank taxon is E. coli562; the exact reviewed sequences are K-12/83333 and MetaCyc includes MG1655/511145. The record uses the K-12 protein core. The [2022 primary full text, PMID37850128](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10521649/fullTextXML), Methods 2.4 and Results 3.2/Figure 2, directly tests MG1655-derived SIJ488 glycolysis mutants on glycerol. Removing GloA or Dld abolished growth in the tested bypass context. Its later PMID indexing does not change the 2022 publication date. This supports the complete route in those conditions; it does not establish constitutive flux in unmodified MG1655, prove GloB exclusivity, or measure every enzyme's isolated stereoselectivity. The older MetaCyc 22.5 description's tentative D-lactate fate is preserved as a dated source limit rather than silently presenting it as direct experimental proof.
